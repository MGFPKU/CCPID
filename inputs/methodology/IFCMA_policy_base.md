# IFCMA Policy Base And Related Attributes

Source: OECD (2024), *The IFCMA's Climate Policy Database: Policy Instruments Typology and Data Structure*, Section 3, Annex C, and Annex D.

## Definition

The policy base refers to the combination of agents, activities, and assets subject to regulation under a given policy instrument, as defined by law ("de jure"). Annex C defines it as the combination of agents, activities, and assets regulated by a policy instrument as defined by the instrument's regulatory framework. [Source: p. 16; p. 35]

The policy base determines the exact scope of the policy instrument, including who precisely has to comply with the instrument's legal obligation. [Source: p. 35]

## Role In The IFCMA Data Structure

| Role | Description | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Scope definition | Identifies the agents, activities, and assets to which the instrument applies. | pp. 16, 35 |
| Sub-scheme definition | Exemptions or differentiated treatments of sub-categories of agents, activities, or assets are captured in sub-schemes. | p. 16 |
| GHG emissions mapping | Accurate policy base definition is a prerequisite for accurate mapping of policy instruments to their GHG emission base. | p. 16 |
| GHG emission base | The GHG emission base is defined by the instrument's policy base and the emission source, which may or may not be within the policy base. | p. 39 |

| Component | IFCMA definition | Examples / response options | Source |

## Regulated Asset

The regulated asset is the item targeted by the policy instrument. The paper states that the asset often determines classification of instruments to GHG emission source sectors. To increase consistency, enable filtering, and support analysis across instruments and countries, regulated assets are standardised based on predefined lists where possible. [Source: pp. 16-17]

Annex D provides a non-exhaustive preliminary list of regulated assets. [Source: pp. 41-42]

## Regulated Agent

The regulated agent is the legal entity that must comply with the instrument, is liable for non-compliance, or receives the established benefits or information. The paper notes that the entity legally required to comply may not be the entity that ultimately bears the financial burden of implementation. [Source: p. 17 and fn. 8]

| Regulated agent option | Examples / notes | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Households | May be characterised by thresholds such as above or below a certain income threshold. | p. 17 |
| Firms | May be characterised by thresholds such as above or below a certain production capacity; firm classification can be linked to ISIC. | pp. 17, 39, 43 |
| Governments | Included as a regulated agent response option. | p. 36 |
| Non-governmental organisations | Included as a regulated agent response option. | p. 36 |

## Regulated Activity

The regulated activity refers to the action or activity regulated by the policy instrument. Activities may narrow the policy base. The paper gives annual GHG emissions thresholds as a minimum emissions condition for a tax and notes that some instruments may be defined by activities or actions without specifying an asset or agent. ## Intensity

Intensity reports the legal obligation or benefit established for the regulated agent by the policy instrument. The requirement can be qualitative or quantitative. The database generally captures intensity as stated in the regulatory statute establishing the instrument. [Source: p. 17]

| Instrument example | Intensity example | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon tax | Tax rate per tonne of CO2e emitted. | p. 17 |
| Emissions standard | Emission limit value. | p. 17 |
| Feed-in tariff | Payment received per unit of renewable electricity generated. | p. 17 |
| Information instrument requiring energy audits | Qualitative requirement. | p. 17 |
| Framework standard requiring BAT adoption | Qualitative requirement. | p. 17 |
| Energy efficiency label | Binary intensity: required or not required. | p. 17 |

## Link To GHG Emission Base

Bloc 5 quantifies an instrument's GHG emission base: the GHG emissions the instrument covers through its policy structure. The paper gives a carbon tax on stationary-source emissions as an example; its GHG emission base includes all CO2 emissions originating from agents and operations liable under the tax. The result may be expressed as absolute emissions, such as Mt CO2, or as a share of sector or economy-wide emissions. [Source: p. 18]

## Link To Classification

Bloc 6 classifies each instrument by the IFCMA policy instrument typology and by sectors. The paper states that GHG emissions source sector classification typically depends on the regulated asset, while economic sector classification depends on the regulated agent. [Source: p. 18]

| Classification attribute | Basis stated in paper | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument Category | IFCMA typology, e.g. economic, regulatory, government investment and consumption, information instruments, voluntary approaches. | pp. 18, 39 |
| Instrument Type | IFCMA typology, e.g. subsidies, taxes, trading systems, performance standards, technology standards. | p. 39 |
| Economic sector | Based on the regulated agent; if the agent is a firm, allocated according to ISIC. | pp. 18, 39 |
| Emissions sector | Based on the technology the instrument addresses, often determined through the regulated asset. | pp. 18, 39-40 |
| GHGs affected | GHGs directly regulated or likely to be affected by the instrument. | p. 40 |
