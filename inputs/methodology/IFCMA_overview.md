# IFCMA Climate Policy Database Overview

Source: OECD (2024), *The IFCMA's Climate Policy Database: Policy Instruments Typology and Data Structure*, Inclusive Forum on Carbon Mitigation Approaches (IFCMA) Paper.

## Purpose

The paper presents a framework for a comprehensive database of climate change mitigation policy instruments developed by the Inclusive Forum on Carbon Mitigation Approaches (IFCMA). The database is intended to systematically classify and describe policy instruments across IFCMA members and support policy dialogue, comparative analysis, and empirical analysis. [Source: pp. 4, 8]

The corresponding database is described as a primary technical output of the IFCMA and is expected to include policy information for all IFCMA members. The IFCMA work plan envisages the first version of the database by the end of 2025, with regular updates thereafter. [Source: p. 8]

## Scope

| Scope item | IFCMA formulation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Climate change mitigation policy instruments | Instruments that explicitly intend to reduce or remove GHG emissions. | p. 8, fn. 1 |
| Mitigation-relevant instruments | Instruments that may induce changes in emissions, positive or negative, without necessarily having emission reductions or removals as a primary goal. | p. 8, fn. 1 |
| Climate policy instruments | The paper uses this term for both climate change mitigation and mitigation-relevant instruments. | p. 8, fn. 1 |
| Unit of stocktake | Climate change mitigation and climate change mitigation-relevant policy instruments. | p. 9 |
| Excluded from current typology | Other forms of climate action such as government strategies, targets or commitments, international co-operation mechanisms, or private sector-led initiatives. | p. 10 |

## Framework Components

Constructing a harmonised policy database involves two key components. This paper focuses on the first component. [Source: p. 8]

| Component | Description | Covered in this paper |
|---|---|---|
| Data structure | Coherent organisation of data, including typology, units of analysis, and policy attributes. | Yes |
| Data collection, management, and validation process | Approach and infrastructure requirements for efficient and effective collection, management, and validation. | No; addressed in a separate document |

## Main Outputs Described

| Output | Description | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Policy instrument typology | A functional classification based on the instrument's operating mechanism. | pp. 10-13 |
| Data structure | A coherent system that codifies information into organised data points. | p. 14 |
| Policy attributes | Standardised variables used to describe and characterise instruments. | pp. 14-18, Annex C |
| GHG emission base | Information on the emissions an instrument covers through its policy structure. | pp. 8, 18 |

## Key Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Policy instrument | A tool through which governments encourage, mandate, or guide behaviour to achieve specific objectives by creating rules, offering services, reallocating resources, or providing information. | p. 9 |
| Policy typology | A grouping or classification of policy instruments into predefined categories or classes based on common characteristics, attributes, or criteria. | p. 9 |
| Data structure | A coherent system that codifies information into organised data points. In a policy database, it defines the unit of analysis and the attributes used to classify, describe, and characterise policy instruments. | p. 14 |
| Policy attributes | Data points used to provide information on the nature, design, and status of an instrument. | pp. 14-15 |
| Sub-schemes | Variations of the main policy instrument that capture differentiated requirements, exemptions, compensation, refunds, transitional periods, compliance requirements, or enforcement mechanisms. | p. 14 |
| Policy base | The combination of agents, activities, and assets subject to regulation under a policy instrument, as defined by law. | pp. 16, 35 |
| GHG emissions mapping | Linking a policy instrument to the GHG emissions it directly targets or applies to, i.e. its emission base. | p. 14, fn. 3 |

## Relationship To Existing Frameworks

| Framework or database | Relationship stated in the paper | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Enhanced Transparency Framework (ETF) under the Paris Agreement | The IFCMA database is intended to complement bottom-up processes such as the ETF; attribute selection and definitions align with ETF reporting guidelines. | pp. 8, 15 |
| UNFCCC ETF reporting structures | The typology and data structure are stated to be fully coherent with ETF data reporting structures. | p. 8 |
| IPCC definitions and concepts | The typology and data structure are stated to be fully coherent with definitions and concepts used by the IPCC. | p. 8 |
| OECD PINE Database | Existing OECD database experience informing the framework; attribute definitions are harmonised with PINE where feasible. | pp. 8, 15 |
| CAPMF, FFS, CPET | Existing OECD policy data work informing the framework. | p. 8 |

## Examples Mentioned In The Paper

| Concept | Example | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Policy instrument | Complying with a standard; paying a tax; receiving a subsidy. | p. 9 |
| Policy package vs. policy instrument | A building code may include multiple individual instruments such as performance standards for windows or roofs and technology standards for heating systems. | p. 14 |
| Policy package | United States Inflation Reduction Act of 2022; European Union Fit-for-55 package. | p. 16, fn. 5; p. 34 |
| GHG emission base indicator | A carbon tax applied to emissions from stationary sources can have an emission base expressed as absolute emissions or as a share of sector or economy-wide emissions. | p. 18 |

## Notes On Evolution

The paper states that the structure goes beyond what the first version of the IFCMA Climate Policy Database of 2025 will entail and may evolve further with experience from additional IFCMA pilot studies. [Source: p. 8]

The first version may not include all attributes identified in the paper. Priority attributes are marked in Annex C because they are necessary for GHG mapping and modelling under the IFCMA technical work. [Source: p. 15, Annex C pp. 34-40]
