# IFCMA Policy Instruments Typology

Source: OECD (2024), *The IFCMA's Climate Policy Database: Policy Instruments Typology and Data Structure*, Section 2 and Annex B.

## Typology Basis

The IFCMA typology is a functional classification based on the policy instrument's operating mechanism. The operating mechanism refers to the rules and processes that govern implementation, linked to the legal obligation imposed on regulated economic agents and the expected behavioural change. [Source: p. 10]

The typology distinguishes five major categories: economic instruments, regulatory instruments, government investment and consumption, information instruments, and voluntary approaches. Each category is separated into policy types. [Source: p. 10]

## Main Categories

| IFCMA category | Definition | Instrument types | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic instruments | Instruments that provide a direct monetary incentive on levels of production or consumption, influencing prices or levels of production or consumption at the margin. | Subsidies; Taxes; Trading systems | pp. 11, 30 |
| Regulatory instruments | Instruments that define allowable actions or set limits to drive adoption of desired processes, technologies, products, or outcomes. | Performance standards; Technology standards; Framework standards | pp. 11, 31 |
| Government investment and consumption | Instruments that allocate government financial resources toward capital assets or shape government expenditure choices and decision-making processes. | Public investment; Public procurement; Public appraisal rules | pp. 11, 31-32 |
| Information instruments | Instruments that mandate collection and/or dissemination of information to raise awareness and communicate knowledge to influence behaviour. | Labels; Reporting requirements; Capacity building and public awareness | pp. 12, 32 |
| Voluntary approaches | Agreements between a government authority and one or more private parties to achieve climate objectives or improve performance beyond regulated obligations; may include government frameworks to facilitate private action. | Voluntary information instruments; Voluntary trading systems; Voluntary targets | pp. 12, 32-33 |

## Instrument Types And Definitions

| Category | Instrument type | Definition | Examples | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Economic instruments | Subsidies | Unrequited payments from government to non-government units. Includes current unrequited payments, lump-sum payments, performance-based subsidies, concessional loans, loan guarantees, other financial risk-mitigation mechanisms, and feebates where applicable. | Electric vehicle purchase subsidies; feed-in tariffs or premiums for renewable electricity; fossil fuel price ceilings; motor vehicle feebates; payments for ecosystem services. | p. 30 |
| Economic instruments | Taxes | Compulsory unrequited payments to a government unit. Includes standard tax rates, tax incentives within a tax regime, and fees and charges defined as compulsory requited payments to government levied more or less in proportion to services provided. | Carbon tax; electricity excise tax; fuel excise tax; tax incentives for clean technologies; tax incentives for fossil fuel use; incineration tax. | p. 30 |
| Economic instruments | Trading systems | Systems that change relative costs or prices by regulating trade of permits or certificates. | Cap-and-trade ETS; baseline-and-credit ETS; tradable renewable energy obligations. | pp. 30-31 |
| Regulatory instruments | Performance standards | Standards that mandate a specific outcome while allowing regulated agents to choose technologies and processes to achieve the performance level. | GHG emission standards; air emission standards; fuel economy standards; minimum energy performance standards; speed limits. | p. 31 |
| Regulatory instruments | Technology standards | Standards that require or prohibit a specific input, technology, or production design. | Biofuel blending standards; electric vehicle sales mandates; bans or phase-outs of fossil fuel-based home heating or power generation plants. | p. 31 |
| Regulatory instruments | Framework standards | Standards that prescribe requirements on production processes without mandating specific inputs, technologies, or outcomes; often qualitative and requiring case-by-case application. | Energy or environmental management system requirements; BAT adoption; priority grid access, dispatch, or curtailment rules; protected areas; zoning requirements. | p. 31 |
| Government investment and consumption | Public investment | Spending by a government unit toward fixed capital such as transport and energy infrastructure, nature-based infrastructure, or intangible assets such as R&D. | Public investment in climate R&D, energy infrastructure, or public transport infrastructure. | pp. 12, 32 |
| Government investment and consumption | Public procurement | Instruments that oblige or encourage public agents to consider climate change mitigation impacts when purchasing goods, services, and works. | Green public procurement rules; public procurement of electric vehicles. | pp. 12, 32 |
| Government investment and consumption | Public appraisal rules | Requirements on government decision-making processes and operations without mandating specific inputs, technologies, or outcomes. | Strategic environmental assessment requirements; shadow carbon pricing in public decision-making. | p. 32 |
| Information instruments | Labels | Requirements for regulated agents to receive a label or certificate in exchange for information on environmental performance, characteristics, or attributes. | Energy performance labels for appliances or vehicles. | pp. 12, 32 |
| Information instruments | Reporting requirements | Requirements for agents to submit regular reports, disclosures, or documentation to regulating agents, oversight bodies, or stakeholders. | Energy audit requirements; environmental auditing. | pp. 12, 32 |
| Information instruments | Capacity building and public awareness | Government provision of information to non-government units to disseminate information, raise awareness, and enhance understanding. | Public information campaigns; education; training; award schemes. | pp. 12, 32 |
| Voluntary approaches | Voluntary information instruments | Government-established instruments to facilitate private action toward climate objectives; participation is voluntary. | Voluntary energy efficiency labels; voluntary financial labels; voluntary reporting standards; voluntary audits. | p. 33 |
| Voluntary approaches | Voluntary trading systems | Government-established systems allowing organisations or companies to trade credits or allowances representing emissions reductions or other environmental benefits; participation is voluntary. | Voluntary GHG emission trading systems, including pilot ETS. | pp. 12-13, 33 |
| Voluntary approaches | Voluntary targets | Self-imposed goals set by organisations or companies to achieve climate objectives or improve environmental performance beyond regulatory compliance in collaboration with government. | Voluntary GHG emission reduction targets; voluntary energy performance targets. | p. 33 |

## Exclusions And Clarifications

| Topic | IFCMA clarification | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Voluntary carbon markets | Voluntary carbon markets driven by the private sector are beyond the scope of the IFCMA Climate Policy Database. | p. 12, fn. 2 |
| Regulatory instruments | Instruments mandating provision of information are classified under information instruments; instruments regulating government spending and decision-making are classified under government investment and consumption; voluntary targets and activities are classified under voluntary approaches. | p. 31 |
| Trading systems | Voluntary emission trading systems are classified under voluntary approaches; offset systems not mandated or regulated by government are excluded. | p. 31 |
| Labels | Voluntary labels and certifications are classified under voluntary approaches. | p. 32 |
| Government involvement | Private agreements without government involvement are excluded from voluntary approaches. | p. 32 |
